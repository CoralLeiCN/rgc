# EAT_HACK Retail Futures brief

## Track 2 challenge

Our selected track is **Track 2: Retail Futures**. Build AI that helps a retailer
or brand decide what to do next, or identifies a decision they have overlooked.
Decisions may concern launches, stocking, pricing, promotion, product removal or
which brands deserve attention. Consider the retail environment: shoppers,
location, channel, range, price point, shopping mission and context.

Possible directions include:

- Discovering promising challenger brands or products people want that are
  missing from the market.
- Explaining how a product or strategy could perform differently across stores,
  locations, channels or shopper groups.
- Combining trends, behaviour and wider signals to validate a new product, or
  testing the implications of launching, removing or moving a product.

A strong entry connects a clear problem, a thoughtful approach, a working
product and evidence that it works. Explain the decision it enables and why the
evidence supports it. Generic chatbots, sentiment summaries, recommenders,
static dashboards, basic search agents and a single prompt are insufficient on
their own; they can contribute to a solution with meaningful insight or workflow.

## Submission requirements

Each team may submit one project. Include:

- Project name and team members.
- Track selection: **Retail Futures**.
- A description of around **100–200 words** covering what was built, the problem
  or opportunity, why it matters and substantial work completed beforehand.
- A video URL for a demo of **at most two minutes**, showing the working product
  and what the team built during EAT_HACK.
- A **public repository URL**, with a short README and instructions for running
  the product.
- The team's **three favourite brands from The Shelf** for the Best Brand vote.

A live product URL is optional and adds no judging points. Every submitted link
must work publicly without signing in or requesting access. The Best Brand vote
is separate from project judging and does not affect the score.

## Judging criteria

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence to demonstrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Originality & Thinking | 30% | A fresh idea or approach with thoughtful reasoning. |
| Build & Execution | 30% | Working core functionality and meaningful technical or product depth. |
| Value & Relevance | 25% | A useful retail or brand decision supported by evidence. |
| Demo & Communication | 15% | A clear explanation of the problem, product and what works today. |
